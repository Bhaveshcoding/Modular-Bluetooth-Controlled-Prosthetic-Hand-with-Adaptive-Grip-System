## Finger Actuation Setup and Basic Movement

### Goal

Use Arduino to make a single tendon-driven robotic finger that can bend and return in a controlled way.

---

### Work Done

* Made a three-part finger out of cardboard for quick prototyping
* Made hinge joints with toothpicks and wire so they could turn smoothly
* Used thread to connect the tendon mechanism to the finger segments
* Added a rubber band so that the fingers could automatically return (extend)
* Attached a servo motor to a stable base structure
* Attached tendon string to servo horn to make it work
* Hooked up the servo to the Arduino Uno (with a signal pin and an external power supply)
* Uploaded and tested basic code for controlling a servo to open and close
* Adjusted the angles of the servos and the tension of the strings to get a wider range of motion.

---

### Issues Faced

* Finger was too stiff at first because the joints were too tight and there was too much friction.
* Routing the tendon made the bending uneven across segments
* The servo's movement was jerky because the angle mapping was wrong
* During testing, the string slipped off the servo horn
* The return mechanism (rubber band) was either too weak or too strong.

---

### Fixes

* Made the holes in the joints a little bigger to lower friction and make things smoother.
* Changed the path of the tendon to make sure that all segments bend the same way
* Set the servo angles so that they work right for opening and closing.
* Tied the tendon down tightly to the servo horn with tighter knots.
* Tried out different levels of tension on the rubber bands to keep the movement even

---

### Result

Used a servo motor to control finger movement successfully. When you press the button, the finger bends smoothly and goes back to where it was before. This proves that the main mechanical and actuation system can be used on all five fingers.
